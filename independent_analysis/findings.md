# Cross-case findings (final AI analysis)

**Status: final AI analysis, 2026-11-10; awaiting human verification.** Built from the 24 final case files in
`cases/` (each made from the clip transcripts and fresh research, then checked against the team's news-only draft)
and the table `comparison.csv`. Counts refer to the 24 cases. With 24 cases chosen because they *became*
controversies, these are patterns among controversies, not risks of joke topics in general (limitation L1).

---

## 1. The answer in one paragraph

Among these 24 controversies, the topic of a joke does not decide how the controversy ends. **Three conditions,
judged from the joke and the political setting before anyone reacts, predict 19 of the 24 outcomes**
(`analysis/conditions.md`): a controversy escalated when the joke mocked a leader whose party holds power (7 of 7),
targeted a group with a statutory defender (5 of 6), or touched the sacred of a group other than the comedian's own
(4 of 5; 0 of 2 when comedians joked about their own family or community). Parties, commissions, police and activist
groups are the **mechanism** that turns these conditions into outcomes, not the explanation (explaining escalation by
"someone with standing acted" would be circular). The conditions in more detail: the joke touches something a group holds **sacred and the law protects**
(deities, Ambedkar, the nation, a regional icon, the family); the target is a **leader whose party holds power
where the comedian performs or lives**; or the offended group has a **statutory defender** (the NCW for women, the
SC/ST Act for Dalits, the courts for persons with disabilities). Where the sacred is touched, the **state route**
opens (FIRs under "hurt sentiments" law, commissions, bans); where it is not, actors with a grievance use the
**street** (threats, assault, disruption) or nothing happens. The clip itself mostly decides **whether** anger
starts, not how far it goes: in most cases the circulated version stripped the frame of the joke, yet restoring the
frame would not have changed the outcome. The punishment, when it came, was **the process** (FIRs in several
states, summons, custody), not a verdict: no case ended in a conviction, and courts limited the process later.

## 3. SQ1: topics, direction and genre

### Topic does not predict the ending

| Topic (cases can have several) | n | Outcomes | Cases |
|---|---|---|---|
| Politics and the state | 10 | both 6 · legal 3 · extra-legal 1 · **online only 0** | agrima, fernandes_2025, jain_univ, kamra_2018, kamra_2025, katikala, munawar, rangeela, sarat, virdas |
| Gender and sexuality | 7 | both 3 · legal 2 · online only 2 | katikala, latent, mohanraj, more_2026, sachdeva, virdas, virli |
| Family | 7 | legal 3 · both 2 · online only 2 | fernandes_2025, katikala, latent, mohanraj, parithabangal, raina_sma, sachdeva |
| Death, violence, tragedy | 8 | legal 4 · online only 3 · both 1 | fernandes_2025, mohanraj, parithabangal, raina_2026, raina_sma, shah, virdas, virli |
| Religion | 4 | legal 2 · extra-legal 1 · both 1 | bharti, fernandes_2024, fernandes_2025, munawar |
| Caste | 3 | both 1 · legal 1 · online only 1 | jain_univ, parithabangal, shah |

- **The same topic ends in opposite ways.** Sex and parents: latent_2025 (FIRs in several states) vs
  sachdeva_2025 (online only). Violence against women and children: virli_2026 (NCW) vs mohanraj_2026 (online only),
  in the same week. Caste: jain_univ_2023 (arrests) vs shah_2021 (online only) vs parithabangal_2025 (a complaint
  that went nowhere).
- **Politics never stayed online (10 of 10).** This is the one topic-level regularity, and section 5 explains it:
  a politician always has someone to act for him.
- **Several topics were assigned in reception, not in the joke.** bharti_2022 never mentions Sikhs (the SGPC
  conceded no community was named); agrima_2020 mocks the statue hype and "masterstroke" talk, not Shivaji;
  faruqui_2024 is a regional crowd-work jibe that a BJP MLA turned religious ("send him to Pakistan"); fernandes_2024
  was a Jain grievance recast by an MLA as Hindu hurt. **What a joke is "about" is partly decided by who claims it.**

### Direction: punching up is riskier, not safer

| Direction | n | both | extra-legal | legal | online only |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| up | 10 | 4 | 2 | 4 | **0** |
| down | 5 | 1 | 0 | 3 | 1 |
| contested / lateral / self | 9 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 4 |

- **No joke punching up stayed online (0 of 10).** In India the powerful target has cadres, fans, friendly police
  or a state institution to respond with (kamra_2025, katikala_2026, agrima_2020, sarat_2026, more_2025).
- **Jokes punching down escalated when the group has a statutory defender** (women: more_2026, virli_2026; Dalits
  plus Ambedkar: jain_univ_2023; children with SMA through an advocacy group in the Supreme Court: raina_sma_2025),
  and stayed online when it did not (shah_2021: no named community, no icon, no state actor).
- **Self-directed or reciprocal jokes stayed online** (sachdeva_2025; raina_2026, where each comic mocked the
  other's region minutes after mocking their own).

### Genre adds little, but dark jokes never went to the street

| Genre | n | both | extra-legal | legal | online only |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| dark | 9 | 2 | **0** | 4 | 3 |
| satire | 12 | 5 | 2 | 4 | 1 |

Dark jokes ended in every outcome except street action alone: they went to commissions and courts or nowhere.
Satire led more often to "both", because satire targets leaders, and leaders have cadres. **Genre matters through
its target, not by itself.** Dark comedy is not riskier as such: of the 9 dark jokes, 3 stayed online.

## 4. SQ2: context, the clip and escalation

### What the clip changed (the main methodological finding)

Reading the full performance against the circulated clip and the news showed that **in most cases the version
the public judged had lost the frame of the joke**:

| Case | What the circulated version left out |
|---|---|
| agrima_2020 | The butt was the statue hype and "masterstroke by Modi ji"; reuploads retitled it "insults Shivaji Maharaj" |
| bharti_2022 | The clip never names Sikhs; reuploads titled it "Sikh beard" |
| sachdeva_2025 | The bit is framed by her coming out as bisexual; the punchline is her family's marriage pressure |
| raina_2026 | Raina had joked about himself as a Kashmiri Pandit minutes earlier, and praised Verma's line on stage |
| fernandes_2025 | Each line quoted in the legal notice is disowned on stage, followed by an anti-violence passage |
| virdas_2021 | The negative lines are paired with positive ones and the piece ends by asking the room to applaud India |
| latent_2025 | The panel immediately mocked Allahbadia's question; the most-viewed clip cuts that reaction |
| sarat_2026 | The Alipiri line the hecklers objected to actually praises Naidu |
| katikala_2026 | The set-up shows the bit is about fan tribalism; the clip keeps his own "divorce is not a joke" disclaimer |
| mohanraj_2026 | The anchor line ("the Haryana for male foeticides") makes it a reversal of son preference; headlines quote a threat she never says |
| faruqui_2024 | A crowd-work generalisation from one audience member's answer; no religious content |
| kamra_2025 | The Shinde song is a short, hedged item; harsher lines on Modi in the same special drew nothing |

In one case the full version was **worse** than the clip (more_2026: the story continues past the viral line to the
woman's repeated refusals, More's own tags, a re-enactment, and More paying the man and asking for applause). In
two cases the news **misattributed the joke's source** (virli_2026: not in *Love & Latex*; raina_sma_2025: from the
2022 special *NARAK*, not *India's Got Latent*). In munawar_2021 there was **no recording at all**: an older 2020
video stood in for jokes nobody recorded.

**But restoring the frame would not have changed most outcomes.** bharti_2022 led to FIRs although no community was
named; sachdeva_2025 stayed online although its frame was lost. **Context collapse explains why outrage starts,
not how far it goes.** A second mechanism matters as much as the cut: **relabelling by reuploaders** (the
retitle "insults Shivaji Maharaj", "Sikh beard", "stand-up terrorism") did the work of labelling before any
objector spoke (Becker).

### Collusion vs collision does not predict escalation

| Circulation | n | both | extra-legal | legal | online only |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| collusion (the comic posted it) | 14 | 4 | 2 | 5 | 3 |
| collision (resurfaced by others) | 9 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 2 |
| live only | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

Escalation rates are about the same. What collision changes is **who frames the joke**: every resurfaced case had a
resurfacer with a stake or a wave behind it (agrima_2020 by an ex-Shiv Sena activist; sarat_2026 by TDP supporters
during a wave of action against Telugu comics; virli_2026 and mohanraj_2026 during the Pranit More wave;
raina_sma_2025 during the *Latent* panic). virli_2026's clip had circulated since June 2025 with little reaction:
**the same clip escalated only when a wave made it legible as part of a pattern.**

## 5. SQ3: who took it up, through which channel, and stigma

### The sacred opens the state route

| Sacred object touched? | n | both | extra-legal | legal | online only |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| yes | 12 | **6** | **0** | 4 | 2 |
| no | 12 | 1 | **4** | 4 | 3 |

- **All four street-only cases touched no sacred object** (faruqui_2024, fernandes_2024, more_2025, sarat_2026):
  a regional jibe, a joke on a Jain practice, a roast of an actor, a roast of a chief minister. With no protected
  object, there was no "hurt sentiments" provision to file under, and grievance went to threats, assault and
  disruption.
- **Six of the seven "both" cases touched the sacred** (Shivaji, Ambedkar, deities, the nation, the family). The
  law encodes the sacred (IPC 295A / BNS 299, "national honour", obscenity and the family), so touching it gives a
  claimant a legal tool on top of the street.
- **The sacred is not sufficient.** sachdeva_2025 and mohanraj_2026 touched the family and stayed online: nobody
  with standing claimed them. And **the sacred is often claimed rather than found** (bharti_2022, agrima_2020,
  faruqui_2024). Durkheim's sacred works here as a resource that actors invoke.

### Who took it up decides the route

| Who took it up | Typical route | Cases |
|---|---|---|
| A party in power where the comic performs, with cadres | police and street together | kamra_2025, katikala_2026, agrima_2020, munawar_2021 (linked activist group) |
| Party supporters outside their state, or an opposition MLA | street only | sarat_2026 (TDP in Karnataka), fernandes_2024 (Hyderabad MLA), faruqui_2024 |
| A statutory body (NCW) | commission and FIRs | more_2026, virli_2026; also latent_2025 |
| A religious or caste body with a legal tool | FIRs | bharti_2022 (SGPC), jain_univ_2023 (SC/ST Act, state department complainant) |
| An advocacy group in court | court | raina_sma_2025 |
| A public institution acting on a complaint | administrative | kamra_2018 (state university) |
| Fans | assault | more_2025 |
| A business | complaint | rangeela_2021 |
| Individuals without an organisation | private notice | fernandes_2025 |
| Objectors with no ally in government | complaint goes nowhere | parithabangal_2025 |
| Nobody with standing | online only | kapil_2024, mohanraj_2026, raina_2026, sachdeva_2025, shah_2021 |

Two contrasts show that **jurisdiction** matters:
- **katikala_2026 vs sarat_2026** (same weeks, Andhra politics). Katikala mocked a Deputy CM whose party governs
  Andhra Pradesh: police complaint, cross-state arrest. Sarat performed in Bengaluru, outside the TDP's state: a stage
  invasion and a forced apology, with no complaint.
- **parithabangal_2025.** Dominant-caste lawyers filed a complaint, but the Tamil Nadu government and Dalit and
  Dravidian groups backed the sketch, so it went nowhere.

### Why some jokes found a claimant and others did not

This is the real question. "Online-only cases are the ones nobody acted on" is true by definition. Across the
fizzled cases, a claimant was missing for one of four reasons:
1. **No organised defender for the offended group** (mohanraj_2026: violence against a male child; no institution
   speaks for men as a group).
2. **The joke was self-directed or reciprocal** (sachdeva_2025; raina_2026).
3. **A powerful target chose not to act** (kapil_2024: Atlee answered gracefully and stayed silent).
4. **No sacred icon and no named community** (shah_2021: "quota" mocked as a category; a quick apology).

Politicians *speaking* is not the same as acting: raina_2026 drew statements from many politicians, but nobody
filed anything.

### Stigma

- Online-only cases stopped at step 1 (labelling). Of the 19 escalated cases, 8 reached step 4 (arrest, custody, a
  ban, assault or venue vandalism): **all 7 "both" cases** and more_2025 (assault). **No case with legal action
  alone reached step 4**: the legal route on its own produced summons and FIRs, while arrest, bans and violence came
  when a party or group acted through police and street together.
- **Courtesy stigma** reached venues, organisers, co-performers or audience members in 11 cases. Examples: a
  demolition at The Habitat (kamra_2025), a vandalised venue (agrima_2020), a venue owner named in an FIR and an
  audience member fired (more_2026), the petrol-pump owner (rangeela_2021).
- **Labelling of the person rather than the joke** was strongest for comedians who already carried a label
  (munawar_2021: jailed for jokes nobody recorded; kamra_2018: cancelled on the label "anti-national" with no joke
  cited; faruqui_2024: a Muslim comic's regional jibe read as religious).

### Apologies and refusals

- **Forced apologies ended street episodes** (faruqui_2024 within a day; sarat_2026 on stage) but **never stopped a
  legal process once it had started** (more_2026, virli_2026, bharti_2022, katikala_2026).
- **Refusals ended the matter when no organised actor was involved** (fernandes_2025), and **prolonged it when one
  was** (virdas_2021, kamra_2025).

## 6. Typology of escalation paths

1. **Statutory path:** a clip about women, Dalits or disability → a statutory body or a protected-group body acts →
   FIRs, summons, court (more_2026, virli_2026, latent_2025, jain_univ_2023, bharti_2022, raina_sma_2025).
2. **Ruling-party path:** a joke about a leader whose party governs locally → cadres and a party-linked complaint →
   police action and street action together (kamra_2025, katikala_2026, agrima_2020, munawar_2021, virdas_2021).
3. **Street path:** an aggrieved group or a politician without the local state → threats, disruption, assault,
   forced apology (sarat_2026, faruqui_2024, fernandes_2024, more_2025).
4. **Thin legal path:** a complaint, notice or cancellation with no follow-through (rangeela_2021, fernandes_2025,
   parithabangal_2025, kamra_2018).
5. **Fizzle path:** online anger, no claimant with standing → fades within about a week (kapil_2024, mohanraj_2026,
   raina_2026, sachdeva_2025, shah_2021).

## 8. Counter-examples

- **fernandes_2025** touched the nation and the Pahalgam dead, yet drew only a private notice: the sacred is not
  enough without an organised claimant.
- **rangeela_2021** mocked the Prime Minister, yet the only action came from an oil supplier pressing a petrol-pump
  owner: politics escalates when a party has a local stake, not merely when a leader is mocked.
- **raina_2026**: many politicians spoke, nobody filed. Criticism is not mobilisation.
- **parithabangal_2025**: a complaint was filed, but the state was on the comedians' side.
- **kapil_2024**: the target, not an objector group, decided; he did not act.

## 9. Answers to the research questions (short)

- **Which topics bring comedians under scrutiny?** Among our controversies: jokes about **leaders and the state**
  (never stayed online), the **family and sexuality**, and **gender violence**. But the topic matters through
  **who can claim it and with what tool**, and several topics were assigned by the people who claimed the joke.
- **Why do controversies end so differently?** Because the route depends on the claimant: **statutory bodies →
  legal; a party governing locally → legal and street; aggrieved groups or politicians without the local state →
  street; nobody with standing → online only.** Touching a **sacred object protected by law** opens the state route;
  without it, grievance goes to the street or fades.
- **How do we treat context?** We compared the full performance with the circulated clip and the news in every case.
  The frame was usually lost, but **context collapse explains the start of outrage, not its course**; relabelling by
  reuploaders and resurfacing during waves matter as much.

## 10. Limits

- 24 cases, all of them controversies: patterns, not general risks. Several cells have 1–3 cases.
- Final AI analysis: every fact and every coding still needs human verification (see `human_checks.md`). Machine
  captions are noisy; tone, gestures and the room are not visible in a transcript.
- Comments have not been collected yet, so whether there was detectable coordinated activity in the YouTube comments is untested; even when tested, the audit cannot show that outrage was organic.
