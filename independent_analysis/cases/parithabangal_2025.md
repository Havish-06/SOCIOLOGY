## Summary

Parithabangal is a Tamil YouTube comedy channel run by Gopi and Sudhakar (with writer-performer Dravid Selvam), with about 6.85 million subscribers. On 4 August 2025, about a week after the killing of Kavin Selvaganesh, a 27-year-old Dalit software engineer, in Tirunelveli on 27 July (allegedly by the brother of the dominant-caste woman he loved), they posted "Society Paavangal" ("society's sins/pitiables"), a 15-minute sketch. It mocks "dominant group" caste pride: invented glorious lineages, caste flex banners, Guru Pooja vehicle convoys, and caste leaders who keep young men idle as foot soldiers. It then stages an honour killing in which a leader goads a boy into killing his sister's lover and abandons him after he surrenders, ending with lines that mock the attack ("throwing chilli powder... and calling it valour") and the social-media videos that sympathised with the accused. The sketch names no caste, but on 6 August a Thevar/Mukkulathor lawyer, joined by reportedly 30+ lawyers, complained to the Coimbatore police commissioner that it defamed the community and incited violence, asking for the video's deletion, a ban on the channel and the creators' arrest; a film producer called them "YouTube clowns". Dalit and Dravidian groups (VCK leader Thirumavalavan, DK/DVK) praised the sketch, filed a counter-complaint and asked for police protection. No FIR, arrest or apology was found; the video stayed up and passed 9.8 million views. The case is a caste sketch that punched up, posted by the creators themselves, whose objectors had standing but no state ally, so it ended at a complaint.

- **Best explanation of this outcome:** The sketch punched up at dominant-caste pride in the week after a widely condemned honour killing, so it sat on the side of the state's own action and of the main Tamil political mainstream. Objectors recognised themselves despite the absence of names and had organisation (a lawyers' association), but they had no ally in power and faced active counter-mobilisation by Dalit and Dravidian groups; the police reviewed the complaint and nothing followed. The creators did not apologise, and with no legal step to resist, the case faded within about two weeks.
- **Topic:** caste, through honour killing. What made it sensitive was not a fixed sacred object but community honour and a community commemoration (Guru Pooja). The dark genre (re-enacting a real killing a week later) gave the objectors a "this incites violence" frame, but the timing also made the sketch read as moral commentary rather than mockery.
- **Rival explanations ruled out, and why:**
  - Fame protects: partly true (very large audience, celebrity backers), but the same duo did apologise and delete in 2024 when the objector was the BJP [6]; fame alone did not protect them then. The difference is the political position of the objectors.
  - Elections: the Tamil Nadu election was about eight months later; no party used the case.
  - Context collapse: ruled out: no clip, no resurfacing; the whole sketch was the object.
  - Platform: YouTube did not remove the video despite reported mass reporting [4][1].
