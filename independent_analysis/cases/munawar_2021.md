## Summary

On 1 January 2021 Munawar Faruqui was due to perform at Monroe Cafe in Indore, Madhya Pradesh. Eklavya Singh Gaur, convenor of the Hind Rakshak Sangathan and son of BJP MLA Malini Gaur, had bought tickets with associates. He stopped the show, took the microphone, and with his group took Faruqui and the organisers to Tukoganj police station, reportedly roughing them up. Police booked Faruqui and four others (later six accused) under IPC 295A, 298, 269, 188 and 34 for allegedly insulting Hindu deities and Home Minister Amit Shah. Within days the police said they had **no video** of Faruqui making such jokes. The town inspector said the complainant's video showed a different comic joking about Ganesh, and the SP said Faruqui "had not yet performed" but was "going to", citing an April 2020 video. Lower courts and the Madhya Pradesh High Court (28 January) refused bail. The Supreme Court granted interim bail on 5 February, calling the FIR's allegations vague, and stayed a UP production warrant from a separate 2020 complaint. He was released late on 6 February after 37 days in jail. The case is **both** legal and extra-legal. It is a "live_only" case in which the jokes punished were never shown to have been told. What circulated as "the joke" was an older clip from April 2020, whose most pointed line ("directed by Amit Shah") explains why the Home Minister appeared in the allegations.

- **Best explanation of this outcome:** An organised Hindutva group led by a ruling-party MLA's son entered the show intending to find offence (**mobilisation; strategic offence**). It stopped the show and handed the comic to police in a BJP-ruled state, where the police and the lower courts treated his **identity and past material as proof of intent** (**identity and power; stigma and labelling**). The legal process itself, through repeated bail refusals and a second-state warrant, was the punishment (**law as a weapon**) until the Supreme Court intervened. Because the case was **live_only**, there was no clip to argue over, so the outcome was driven by who controlled the room and the police station, not by what was said.
- **Topic:** Religion (majority deities) combined with a political leader (Amit Shah) and a communal tragedy (Godhra). These made the comedian's earlier material highly sensitive. At Indore the topic was alleged rather than performed. The topic worked as a label attached to the comedian, not a property of a joke.
- **Rival explanations ruled out, and why:** *The joke itself*: ruled out; the police admitted no evidence [2][3], and the SP said he had not performed [4]. *Fame*: he was mid-level, and the case made him famous rather than the reverse. *Platform/virality*: the arrest came before any viral clip of the Indore show (there was none). *Elections*: no election in MP at the time. *COVID rules alone*: these would not explain 295A or custody for 37 days.
