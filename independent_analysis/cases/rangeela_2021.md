## Summary

On 16 February 2021, mimic Shyam Rangeela posted a two-minute video, shot at a petrol pump in Sri Ganganagar (Rajasthan), in which he imitates Prime Minister Narendra Modi. In Modi's speech style he "celebrates" petrol reaching ₹100 a litre, telling the people of Sri Ganganagar to swell their chests with pride. Petrol in Sri Ganganagar crossed ₹100 for the first time in India on 17 February 2021, a day after the upload, so the video caught a national news peg. The video spread widely: about a million YouTube views within days. On 19 February the owner of the pump, Surendra Agarwal, complained at Sadar police station that Rangeela had posed as a journalist and asked only to take photographs. Agarwal said his oil supplier company had pressed him to file the complaint and had threatened to halt his fuel supply. Rangeela replied on video that he had permission. He refused to delete the video, apologised to the pump owner for the trouble caused, but would not apologise for the content. No FIR, arrest or court action against Rangeela was confirmed. The main harm fell on the venue, a business, not the comedian. This was punishment through a corporate supply chain, not through a party or the street.
