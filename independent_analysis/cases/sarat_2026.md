## Summary

Hyderabad comedian Sarat Uday posted an eight-minute Telugu set, "Andhra Politics", in December 2024, recorded at a Bengaluru club. It mocked politicians of all three major Andhra parties: the YSRCP's former IT minister, Pawan Kalyan of the Jana Sena, and the TDP's Nara Lokesh and Chandrababu Naidu. Rival party supporters clipped and shared the parts that hurt the other side. Both sides threatened him, and by late December 2024 he had apologised, muted the Pawan Kalyan jokes and said he would stop political comedy. In April 2026, after police arrested fellow comedian Anudeep Katikala over Pawan Kalyan jokes, TDP and Jana Sena supporters recirculated his old video. One X account listed his upcoming Bengaluru show and asked the Andhra police to act. On 25 April 2026, about a dozen self-described TDP supporters walked on stage at Ministry of Comedy, Koramangala, a few minutes into his solo show. They demanded an apology to Naidu, Lokesh and TDP cadres, asked him to chant party slogans, and warned him ("final warning"). He refused the slogans but apologised again. The venue called the police, and the group left before they arrived. A pro-TDP YouTube channel then celebrated the episode as "Cadre Power". The TDP state president condemned the disruption but said comedians who cross the line should face *legal* action. The clip suggests that the line the hecklers objected to, the 2003 assassination attempt on Naidu, actually *praises* Naidu's toughness and is set up to deflate his son Lokesh.

- **Best explanation of this outcome:** this is a **collision in a wave**. An old, even-handed political set, already apologised for, was pulled back into circulation because a coalition partner (JSP) had just shown that jokes about its leader bring police action. The show was in Bengaluru, outside the Andhra police's reach and with no party office-bearer filing a complaint, so local TDP supporters took the **extra-legal** route: stage invasion, a forced apology and a slogan test of loyalty. The party leadership then disowned the method but endorsed the legal route. The difference from Katikala's case is the route available (street vs police), not the topic.
- **Topic:** politics_state and leader_honour, with a reference to violence against the leader (Alipiri). The roast-of-everyone format lost its even-handedness once clipped: each party's supporters saw only the jokes about their own leader.
- **Rival explanations ruled out, and why:**
  - *Severity of the joke*: the hecklers' main grievance (Alipiri) concerns a line that praises Naidu; severity does not explain the timing.
  - *Fame*: a small channel; the clip's reach came from a partisan channel after the event.
  - *Election*: none.
  - *Comedian's new material*: there was none. He says he had stopped political jokes, so the trigger was external (the wave).
