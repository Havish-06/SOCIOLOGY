## Summary

In April 2019 Agrima Joshua performed a short bit at a comedy cafe in Khar (West), Mumbai, generally identified as The Habitat. In it she mocked what Quora answers said about the planned Shivaji Maharaj memorial statue in the Arabian Sea: that it was a "masterstroke" by Prime Minister Modi, would run on solar cells, would carry a GPS tracker and would shoot lasers at Pakistani terrorists. On 9 July 2020 a former Shiv Sena activist posted the clip and asked the police to take the "strictest action". On 10 July a Shiv Sena MLA, Pratap Sarnaik, wrote to Maharashtra's Home Minister demanding her arrest. Joshua took the video down and apologised the same day. Over the following night, MNS activists damaged the stage and furniture at the cafe. On 11 July the Home Minister directed the police and the cyber cell to "take legal action expeditiously", and Joshua posted a second apology in Hindi addressed to members of the NCP, Shiv Sena, Congress and MNS. She received rape and death threats. Police arrested the men who made them, and two days later the police said they were still seeking a legal opinion on acting against her. No FIR against Joshua was found. The case ended with **both** legal pressure (complaint and ministerial direction) and extra-legal action (vandalism, threats, pressured apology). The clip shows a joke whose butt is the online hype and the state project, not Shivaji. Once it was retitled "insults Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj", its target was reframed.

- Topic(s): regional_national_icon; politics_state
- Genre(s): satire_horatian
- Why this genre: the laugh comes from the absurd escalation of online claims about a costly state monument and from the "masterstroke" phrase that is used to praise the PM. The mockery is playful and aimed at political hype and gullible online sources, not contemptuous of a powerful person. There is no grave subject whose gravity produces the laugh, so it is not dark.
- Target: the planned Shiv Smarak statue project and the online and political hype around it (Quora posts, "masterstroke by Modi ji"). The objectors read the target as Shivaji Maharaj himself.  Direction: up (a state mega-project and the PM's image). Objectors reframed it as an attack on a sacred regional icon.

- Main technique: exaggeration (escalating list of absurd claims read out "from Quora"), with irony

  - state direction: the Home Minister instructed the police commissioner and IG Cyber to "take legal action expeditiously" (11 July) [1][2]. Police sought a legal opinion (13 July) [8][9].

  - legal basis: none formally invoked. Implied religious_offence/defamation of an icon.
- Extra-legal actions: vandalism of the venue by MNS activists [1][2][3]; rape and death threats [2][3][5]; pressured apologies (two public apologies within 24 hours; the second addressed to four political parties by name [7]; a written apology to MNS reportedly extracted at the venue).
- Highest stigma step reached: 3 status loss (video taken down; venue attacked; calls for a ban on such comedians). Threats of violence reached step 4 in intent, but no ban, arrest or violence against her person occurred, so I code 3.
- Courtesy stigma: yes. The venue (The Habitat, Khar) was vandalised and its staff's details were shared online [1].
- How it ended: no FIR. Political attention moved on after about a week. The criminal process ran against the men who threatened her, not against her.
- Winners and losers: Shiv Sena and MNS figures gained a visible role as defenders of Shivaji's honour, inside a coalition that had just lost the "Hindutva" claim to the BJP. The NCW and the police gained credit for acting on the threats. Joshua lost the video and faced abuse, and the venue suffered damage. Over time she gained public sympathy as a target of misogynistic threats.

- **Best explanation of this outcome:** A third-party political activist resurfaced the clip (**context collapse / collision**) and reframed it as an insult to Shivaji, a protected regional sacred figure (**Durkheim**). Competing Marathi-identity parties then took it up. The Shiv Sena, in government, used the formal channel (letter, ministerial direction), and MNS cadres used the street channel (**mobilisation; strategic offence**). This produced both legal pressure and extra-legal action within 48 hours. Her immediate apologies, together with the media's turn to the rape threats against her, stopped the legal route at a "legal opinion".
- **Topic:** The sensitive element was the sacred regional icon Shivaji, not the PM. The genre (gentle satire of online hype) did not protect it, because the sacred name appeared in a laughing context once the frame was removed.
- **Rival explanations ruled out, and why:** *Fame*: she had little fame, yet it escalated fast, so fame does not explain the escalation. *Elections*: no election was near (Maharashtra voted in Oct 2019). *Timing wave*: other comedians' old clips were trolled in July 2020 [10], which may have raised attention, but the decisive actors were Sena/MNS. *Platform*: the clip's YouTube reuploads amplified it but did not start it. *Gender*: it explains the form of the threats, not the political escalation.

**Setup and follow-up.** The bit is built as a reading of internet answers: setup (an "amazing" project in Mumbai), then the "authentic source" (Quora, ironic), then escalating claims (masterstroke by Modi; power for Maharashtra; a second answer adding more "creativity"; lasers against Pakistanis). The butt of each step is the credulous, nationalist online writer and the political branding of the project. The line "not Shivaji, Shivaji Maharaj" is a correction of the honorific. From the captions alone I cannot tell whether she corrects herself, quotes a heckler, or uses it as a tag that mocks people who police the honorific. **This is the most important point for a human to watch,** because it decides whether the clip contains any jab at Shivaji's devotees at all.
