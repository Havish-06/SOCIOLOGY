## Summary

About a month after the 22 April 2025 Pahalgam attack, and days after Operation Sindoor and the 10 May ceasefire, Daniel Fernandes uploaded a 23-minute club set to his YouTube channel. The set satirises the government's response, TV news, online war-mongering and anti-Pakistan hate. It ends with an absurd "plan" for nuclear war, after which there would be "no Hindus, no Muslims, only mutants". On 29 May 2025 two Supreme Court advocates, Makarand D. Adkar and Amita Sachdeva, describing themselves as "concerned citizens", sent him a legal notice. It listed six timestamped passages, plus an older X post of jokes about parents, and demanded removal by 3 June. They said the content insulted the Home Minister, trivialised the attack and harmed India's image. On 4 June Fernandes made the notice public and politely refused, citing Article 19(1)(a) and calling the set "absurdist satire". No FIR, court case, threat or cancellation has been found. The video is still online. The outcome is **legal** in the narrow sense (a private legal notice) and went no further. The case is notable because the most dangerous-sounding material (a terror attack, the army, war) produced the mildest response of this comedian's two controversies.
