## Summary

Students of the Centre for Management Studies (CMS) of Jain (Deemed-to-be) University in Bengaluru staged a seven-minute skit in the "Mad Ads" event of the university's Youth Fest '23 in early February 2023. The skit, by a group called "The Delroys Boys", played on caste and reservation with lines such as "Why be Dalit when you can be D-Lit" and a renaming of B.R. Ambedkar as "Beer Ambedkar". Students who watched it complained to the organisers, were dismissed, and then started an online petition; an edited video spread online around 9–10 February. On 10 February the university suspended six students, the group apologised on Instagram, and police in Bengaluru registered an FIR under the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act and IPC sections 153A and 295A on a complaint by an official of the state Social Welfare Department (an activist of the Vanchit Bahujan Aghadi youth wing had also complained in Maharashtra). On 13 February police arrested seven students, the dean and a programme coordinator, who were remanded in police custody and later bailed. The Karnataka High Court stayed the case in July 2023 and quashed it in early 2025, calling the skit satire protected by Article 19 and the prosecution an abuse of process. The case is the clearest example in this set of a joke that punched down at a protected group and touched a sacred icon (Ambedkar) moving quickly through a statutory route to arrest.

- **Best explanation of this outcome:** The skit punched down at Dalits and touched Ambedkar, a sacred icon, in front of Dalit students who were present. Their objection met a ready statutory channel (the SC/ST Act, with a state department willing to file), so the case went from petition to FIR to arrest within days. The arrests, not a conviction, were the punishment: the High Court later found no intent and quashed the case. Institutional pressure (suspension) and a quick apology did not slow the legal process because the law, not the comedians' response, drove it.
- **Topic:** caste with a sacred icon (Ambedkar) and a protected group (Dalits). The dark wordplay made the lines easy to quote out of context and hard to defend as "satire" once quoted. The college setting (students, an institution) gave the authorities targets (dean, coordinator) beyond the performers.

### Agreement
Both analyses agree on the following. Jain University CMS students staged a "Mad Ads" skit at the university youth fest in early February 2023. It renamed Ambedkar "Beer Ambedkar" and mocked Dalits. Students who had seen it petitioned against it and the clip spread online (a collision, with a gap of 0 months). Police registered an FIR at Siddapura police station under the SC/ST (PoA) Act and the IPC, and on 13 February arrested seven students, the dean and the programme coordinator. The Karnataka High Court stayed the case in July 2023 and quashed it in about March 2025. Both code the topic as caste with Ambedkar as the sacred object, the direction as down, the norm as caste_dignity, the highest stigma step as 4, courtesy stigma as yes, and the election period as yes. Both read the case mainly through law as a weapon, mobilisation through a dedicated statute, and Durkheim.

### Errors found
- In the team draft: F says "extra-legal actions: none found" although its own G records the university suspension, so the outcome should be both. It places the Law Minister's "cannot be withdrawn on the skit alone" remark in August 2023, but the remark was made in February 2023 under the BJP government; the August 2023 request came from the new Congress government. It suggests Kannada or Hindi, but the language is English. It names activists or students as the FIR complainant, but the Bengaluru complainant was a state official. "amateur" is not a codebook tier.
- In the independent analysis: the Coding summary gives `form: sketch` but B gives `college_skit`; the codebook form for this case is college_skit. It misses the 14 days of judicial custody, the state's August 2023 deemed-status letter, and the Karnataka Congress and JD(S) demands. It did not describe the storyline.

### What the clip changed
The clip confirms the "Why be Dalit when you can be D-Lit" line. It also shows applause in the room right after the opening caste puns, so the live audience accepted the comic frame and the offence came from Dalit students who were present and then from wider publics. The clip also shows reservation and untouchability jokes (the "General" pun, "don't touch that water") that news reports barely quote, which supports adding politics_state (reservation) to the topic. It cannot show the claimed anti-caste ending, so the performers' "satire" defence, which the High Court later accepted from a full transcript, cannot be judged from the clip.
