## Summary

In a 46-minute YouTube special, "Naya Bharat", Kunal Kamra sang a parody of "Bholi Si Surat" (from *Dil To Pagal Hai*). The song describes, without naming him, a politician from Thane with a beard and glasses who hid in Guwahati and "looks like a traitor" (*gaddar*): clearly Maharashtra Deputy CM Eknath Shinde. Kamra uploaded the special on 23 March 2025 and posted the clip on X himself. A Shiv Sena (UBT) MP then shared it. That night, workers of Shinde's Shiv Sena vandalised the Habitat studio in Khar, Mumbai, where the show had been filmed. Next day a Shiv Sena MLA's complaint led to an FIR for defamation and public mischief. The municipal corporation (BMC) also demolished parts of the studio as "unauthorised". More FIRs, three police summonses, a breach-of-privilege motion in the Legislative Council and his delisting from BookMyShow followed. Kamra refused to apologise. He obtained interim anticipatory bail from the Madras High Court, and on 25 April 2025 the Bombay High Court barred his arrest while letting the investigation continue. The clip shows that the Shinde song is one short item in a set that attacks Modi, the BJP and the RSS far more harshly. The backlash came from the one target with a local, organised cadre in Mumbai.

## F. Outcome
- Outcome type: **both**
- Legal actions against the comedian:
  - FIR at MIDC police (zero FIR), transferred to Khar: complainant Shiv Sena MLA Murji Patel, a member of the target's party, speaking for the leader and party [2][3]. BNS 353(1)(b), 353(2) (public mischief / statements conducing to it), 356(2) (defamation). Legal basis: **defamation**; other (public mischief).
  - About three more FIRs from elsewhere in Maharashtra (reported) [8][16].
  - Three summonses under BNSS 35(3) [2][16].
  - Breach-of-privilege notice in the Maharashtra Legislative Council by BJP MLC Pravin Darekar; committee hearings 2026 [12][21][22].
  - **Administrative:** BMC demolition at the venue the day after (against the venue, but triggered by the joke) [9][10].
  - Court: Kamra's own petitions gave him protection (Madras HC 28 Mar 2025; Bombay HC 25 Apr 2025).
- Extra-legal actions: vandalism of the venue (23 Mar); public threats by MP Mhaske and leader Nirupam [3]; death threats that Kamra reported [2]; delisting by BookMyShow after a party leader's letter [17][18]; demands for an apology (refused).
- Highest stigma step reached: **4** (violence against the venue; FIRs with summonses; a platform effectively banning him from ticketing). Labelling ("supari", "traitor to Mumbai's culture" type language) at step 1; ticketing exclusion at step 3.
- Courtesy stigma: **yes**. Habitat studio vandalised, partly demolished and closed. The hotel was ransacked. BookMyShow pulled his listings. Audience members were reportedly contacted by police (disputed).
- How it ended: legally unresolved as of the last sources found. No arrest; the Bombay HC bars arrest and cognisance of any chargesheet while the quashing petition is pending; privilege proceedings were continuing in April 2026.
- Winners and losers: Shinde's Sena showed cadre strength and defended its leader's honour, but also drew criticism for vandalism. The Uddhav Sena gained a stick to beat Shinde with ("gaddar" stuck). Kamra gained enormous views (19+ million) and a free-speech profile, but lost Mumbai as a base (he lives in Tamil Nadu) and lost BookMyShow ticketing. The Habitat, a long-standing comedy venue, lost most.

## G. Comedian's response
- Response: **refusal** (no apology; cooperated with lawful process; went to court; kept publishing).
- When: within about 24 hours (late 24 March 2025), after the vandalism, FIR and demolition had already happened [5][6]. Restated before the privilege committee in April 2026 [22].
- Did the response change the course of events? **Unclear.** More FIRs and summonses followed the refusal, but the main escalations (vandalism, FIR, demolition) came before it. The courts, not the response, stopped the arrest risk.
- Comedian's own narrative: **free_speech** and defiance. "Your inability to take a joke at the expense of a powerful public figure does not change the nature of my right" [5]. He says he only repeated what Ajit Pawar had said about Shinde [5]. In the special itself, he pre-framed this with the Constitution (44:44).

- **Best explanation of this outcome:** The song attacked a sitting Deputy CM on the most sensitive point of his career (the 2022 "betrayal" of the Thackerays). It went viral through the comedian's own post and a rival faction's amplification. The target's party had cadres in the very city where the venue stood, and allies controlling the state government and the municipal corporation. This combination of **strategic offence**, **mobilisation with political opportunity** and **law as a weapon** produced both outcomes on the same day: cadre vandalism (extra-legal) and an MLA's FIR plus a municipal demolition (legal/administrative). Courts later limited the legal process, but the process itself became the punishment.
- **Topic:** politics_state, a leader's honour. Nothing in the fixed sacred list is touched. What made the topic explosive was *who* the target was (a powerful regional leader with a street-level cadre and a contested legitimacy story), not a sacred object. The roast form (one person, unnamed but unmistakable) made it easier to cast as a personal attack ("supari") than as satire of policy.
- **Rival explanations ruled out, and why:**
  - *Fame/reach alone*: the same special calls Modi a "dictator" and "killer" in song (41:51–44:24) and attacks Nirmala Sitharaman, yet the national BJP did not file FIRs over those lines. Reach does not explain why only the Shinde song was punished. Local mobilisation does.
  - *Election timing*: no election was under way (the assembly election was four months earlier). Pending BMC polls may have raised the stakes for the Shinde Sena, but that is speculative.
  - *Platform*: the same YouTube upload carried all the songs; the platform did not select the Shinde segment.
  - *Comedian's identity*: Kamra's reputation shaped the framing, but the decisive variable was the target's organisation, not who Kamra is.
