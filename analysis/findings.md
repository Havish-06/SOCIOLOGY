## 2. The main answer, in one paragraph

Across 24 controversies, a controversy goes beyond online anger only when an actor with organisation or legal
standing takes the joke up (a party, a statutory body, a religious institution, an organised group). That part is
close to how the outcomes are defined, so it is **not** the finding. The finding is **why such actors took up some
jokes and not others, and which route they chose.** In the draft cases, the answer lies in the topic: it decides
**who has a stake and a tool**. Women and the family have the NCW and obscenity law; Dalits have the SC/ST Act; Sikhs
have the SGPC; political leaders have party workers and friendly police. A joke that touches such a group, and fits
such a tool, was taken up; a joke whose offended group has no defender with a tool (men as a group, mohanraj_2026; a
self-directed regional roast, raina_2026) stayed online even when touching the sacred. The kind of actor then shapes
the route: statutory bodies and protective laws lead to the legal route; parties with cadres lead to threats,
vandalism and disruption, often alongside an FIR. Waves matter too: during a wave, institutions are already primed
(virli_2026).

## 3. SQ1: topics and outcomes

| Topic | online only | legal | extra-legal | both | total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Politics and the state | 0 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 5 |
| Regional and national icons, nation | 1 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 4 |
| Religion | 0 | 1 | 0 | 2 | 3 |
| Caste | 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 3 |
| Gender and sexuality | 1 | 2 | 0 | 0 | 3 |
| Family | 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| Disability | 0 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| National trauma | 0 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| Celebrity | 0 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 1 |
| Looks | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| **Total** | 5 | 7 | 4 | 8 | 24 |

Patterns:
- **Politics and religion never stayed online** (8 of 8 escalated). Jokes about leaders went mostly to the street or
  to both routes (4 of 5); religion went to the courts or both.
- **Gender, disability and national-trauma jokes went to the legal route, never to the street** (5 of 5 escalated
  cases): women and persons with disabilities are defended by statutory bodies and courts, not cadres.
- **The same topic can end opposite ways.** Family: latent_2025 (both) vs sachdeva_2025 (online only). Caste:
  jain_univ_2023 (arrests) vs shah_2021 (online only) vs parithabangal_2025 (complaints only). So topic is necessary
  context, not a sufficient explanation.
- **Direction matters.** Jokes punching **up** at power never stayed online (10 of 10). Jokes where comedians mocked
  **their own** group stayed online (2 of 2). Jokes punching **down** went to the legal route when the target group has a
  statutory defender (4 cases) and stayed online when it did not (shah_2021, where no sacred icon was touched).
- **Genre adds little once topic and mobilisation are known.** Dark jokes appear in every outcome (online only 3, legal 4,
  both 3); crude jokes likewise. Dark comedy on the same show stayed online (raina_2026) while a crude joke escalated
  (latent_2025), but the difference is better explained by legal fit (obscenity) than by genre.

## 4. SQ2: context and escalation

- **Collusion vs collision does not by itself predict escalation**: 11 of 14 collusion cases and 6 of 8 collision cases
  escalated. Both live-only cases escalated.
- **What collision does change is who gets to frame the joke.** Every collision case was resurfaced either during an
  active wave (virli_2026, mohanraj_2026) or by actors with a stake (agrima_2020 by Marathi-identity supporters,
  sarat_2026 by TDP supporters, shah_2021 by anti-caste users). The resurfacer's identity, not the time gap, predicts
  the route.
- **Context travels badly in both directions.** In-room frames were clear in every case where we know the room (studio
  shows, clubs, specials). The comic frame was lost when clips reached audiences at social distance: objectors almost
  always spoke *for* a group (a religion, women, a region, a party), rarely as the joke's own audience.
- **Timing matters through waves.** Two waves are visible: after latent_2025 (sachdeva_2025, raina_sma_2025 before the
  same bench) and after more_2026 (virli_2026, mohanraj_2026). During a wave, institutions are already primed, so
  even old jokes escalate (virli_2026).

## 5. SQ3: who mobilised, through which channel, and stigma

| Kind of actor who took it up | Typical route | Cases |
|---|---|---|
| Statutory bodies, police acting on their own, courts | legal | latent_2025, more_2026, virli_2026, raina_sma_2025 |
| Religious institutions and groups with protective laws | legal | bharti_2022 (SGPC), jain_univ_2023 (SC/ST Act) |
| Ruling party with cadres, inside its own state | both | kamra_2025, katikala_2026, agrima_2020 |
| Party supporters outside their state, or a single politician's threats | extra-legal | sarat_2026, faruqui_2024, fernandes_2024 (with a minor complaint) |
| Hindu nationalist activists and organisations | both, long trail | munawar_2021, virdas_2021, kamra_2018 |
| Fans of a celebrity | extra-legal | more_2025 |
| Nobody with standing | online only | sachdeva_2025, mohanraj_2026, kapil_2024, shah_2021, raina_2026 |

- **Stigma ladder:** all 5 online-only cases stopped at step 1 (labelling). Escalated cases reached steps 1–4; 7 reached
  step 4 (arrests, FIRs in several states, violence).
- **Labelling of the person, not the joke**, is strongest where the comedian already carried a label or minority identity:
  munawar_2021 (jailed without a recorded joke) and faruqui_2024 (a regional jibe answered with "send him to Pakistan");
  kamra_2018 (cancelled on the label "anti-national" with no specific joke).
- **Courtesy stigma** reached venues and audiences in 10 cases (vandalised or demolished venues, audience members summoned,
  co-performers named in FIRs).
- **Whose sacred?** The sacred objects on our fixed list (deities, religious symbols, Shivaji, Ambedkar, the nation, the
  family) were touched in 9 of the 19 escalated cases and in 2 of the 5 online-only cases (both about the family:
  sachdeva_2025, mohanraj_2026). Touching the sacred raises the stakes, but without an actor who acts it still fizzles.

Reading: **scandal, mobilisation and the sacred separate escalated from fizzled cases**; humour theory, context collapse
and platform outrage are present everywhere, so they explain why outrage starts but not how far it goes. **Strategic
offence concentrates in "both" cases**, the ones driven by parties.

## 8. Typology of escalation paths

1. **Statutory path:** viral clip → statutory body or police act on their own → FIRs, summons, court
   (latent_2025, more_2026, virli_2026, raina_sma_2025, bharti_2022, jain_univ_2023).
2. **Party path:** joke about a leader → party workers and a party-linked complaint → vandalism or threats, often with
   an FIR (kamra_2025, katikala_2026, agrima_2020, fernandes_2024, faruqui_2024, sarat_2026).
3. **Activist path:** activist asserts offence or a label → arrest or cancellation, then a long trail of cancellations
   (munawar_2021, virdas_2021, kamra_2018).
4. **Fandom path:** roast of a star → fans attack (more_2025).
5. **Fizzle path:** online outrage, no actor with standing acts → fades within about a week (sachdeva_2025, mohanraj_2026,
   kapil_2024, shah_2021, raina_2026; near-fizzles: fernandes_2025, rangeela_2021).

## 9. Counter-examples

- **rangeela_2021** (politics, satire of the PM) drew only a procedural complaint from a business. Politics escalates
  when a party with cadres has a direct stake; mocking the PM from afar did not trigger one.
- **fernandes_2025** (a terror attack: highly "sacred") drew only a private notice. The sacred is not enough without an
  actor who acts.
- **raina_2026**: politicians criticised it publicly, yet it stayed online. Criticism is not mobilisation: nobody filed a
  complaint.
- **parithabangal_2025**: complaints were filed but the state did not act; counter-mobilisation and the regional political
  culture sided with the satire.
- **Apologies:** they ended the extra-legal episodes (fernandes_2024, faruqui_2024, sarat_2026) but never stopped a legal
  process once a statutory body or police had started (latent_2025, more_2026, virli_2026, katikala_2026).

## 11. What we can and cannot claim (so far)

- We can say how these controversies differ and what goes with each outcome. We cannot say any topic is "risky" in
  general (selection on the outcome, limitation L1).
- Several findings rest on single cases (fandom path; the jurisdiction contrast). Treat them as hypotheses.
- No case ended in a conviction. Where the legal route was used, the process itself (FIRs in several states, travel,
  custody, summons of audiences) was the punishment, and courts usually limited it later.
